What Is PRP Therapy?

PRP therapy uses components of your own blood—specifically platelets rich in growth factors—to stimulate hair follicles. The process involves:

Drawing a small sample of your blood

Spinning it in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets

Injecting the platelet-rich plasma into targeted areas of the scalp

Growth factors in PRP can:

Promote new blood vessel formation around hair follicles

Why Your Scalp Needs Medical Next Steps

PRP can be effective, but it's not a one-size-fits-all solution. Your scalp health and underlying causes of hair loss need professional assessment. Here's why:

1. Identifying the Root Cause

Hair loss may result from:

Androgenetic Alopecia (male or female pattern baldness)

Telogen Effluvium (stress-related shedding)

Alopecia Areata (autoimmune patchy loss)

Hormonal imbalances, nutritional deficiencies, or scalp infections

A dermatologist or trichologist can order blood tests, scalp biopsies, or hormone panels to pinpoint the trigger.

2. Customizing Treatment Plans

Combining PRP with:

Topical Treatments (minoxidil, corticosteroids)

Oral Medications (finasteride for men, spironolactone for women)

Nutritional Support (biotin, iron, vitamin D)

…often yields better results. You need medical guidance to avoid ineffective or redundant therapies.

3. Monitoring Progress Safely

Side effects of PRP are rare but can include:

Mild scalp pain or swelling

Temporary bruising at injection sites

Headaches or lightheadedness

A medical team can monitor and adjust protocols to minimize discomfort and maximize benefit.

4. Preventing Complications

If you have:

Bleeding disorders

Platelet abnormalities

Active scalp infections

Recommended Medical Next Steps

A specialist will take your history, examine your scalp, and recommend tests.

Check thyroid function, iron levels, vitamin D, and hormone profiles.

Dermoscopy or biopsy to look for miniaturization of follicles or inflammation.

Explore adjunct treatments (topicals, supplements, lifestyle adjustments).

Understand typical timelines (3–6 months to see visible regrowth).

Additional Tips for a Healthy Scalp

Use a gentle, pH-balanced shampoo.

Avoid hairstyles that pull on hair (tight buns, ponytails).

Manage stress through exercise, meditation, or therapy.

Eat a balanced diet rich in protein, healthy fats, and micronutrients.

Protect your scalp from UV exposure with hats or SPF sprays.
