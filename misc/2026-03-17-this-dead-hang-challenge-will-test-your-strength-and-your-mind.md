# This Dead Hang Challenge Will Test Your Strength and Your Mind

Is hanging out so hard? For a recent Athlean-X video, Jeff Cavaliere C.S.C.S. and Jesse Laico attempt the "100 seconds, 100 dollars" challenge, which requires you to hang from a bar for—you guessed it—100 seconds. "The arm hang is one of those under-appreciated exercises that kind of sneaks in and just kills you when you do it," says Cavaliere.

The catch here, however, is that the hang challenge employs a rotating bar rather than a static one, making it even more of a test of "grip strength, forearm strength, stamina, and good old-fashioned mental fortitude and toughness," as Cavaliere puts it. When attempting this dead hang challenge yourself, it's important to remember that it's a little more complicated than just hopping up to the bar and hanging on. The key is scapular retraction—once you're holding onto the bar, pull your shoulder blades down and back, and maintain that position throughout the hold. Don't just let your shoulders fall and break your posture to slouch.

To provide a baseline for the challenge, Cavaliere and Laico both dead-hang from straight bars first; Laico maxes out at 2 minutes and 7 seconds, while Cavaliere lasts 2 minutes 51 seconds. However, when it comes to hanging from an Olympic bar placed on top of the rack, which rotates, things get tougher, as it requires them to constantly adjust their grip.

"It's way harder than the regular hang," says Laico. "Oh my god, this is probably 10 times harder... My forearms are burning." He ends up dropping from the bar after 1 minute and 25 seconds, just 15 seconds off the target, and his hands are so tired that he couldn't even grip a $100 bill if he tried.

Cavaliere, meanwhile, passes the 100 second mark, and is actually able to hold on even longer, making it to 1 minute 53—although he admits it's "way harder" than the straight bar version. "If you try it, I promise you, it's tough," he says. "It's a lot tougher when you have the rotating bar in your hands."
