Explanation

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an herb long used in Ayurvedic medicine for stress, energy and overall well-being. Today's research is exploring its potential benefits—and its risks. Here's what doctors and credible clinical studies say about ashwagandha effectiveness, plus safety considerations many people overlook.

What Is Ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha is often called "Indian ginseng" for its adaptogenic qualities—helping the body adapt to physical and mental stress. Available in capsules, powders and extracts, it contains active compounds (withanolides) that may influence inflammation, hormone regulation and the nervous system.

Ashwagandha Effectiveness: Doctor-Backed Findings

Stress and Anxiety Reduction

Multiple randomized controlled trials show ashwagandha can lower cortisol ("stress hormone") by 14–28% over 8–12 weeks.

In one study, adults with chronic stress taking 300–600 mg of a standardized extract twice daily reported significant reductions in perceived stress and anxiety scores versus placebo.

Improved Sleep Quality

Ashwagandha's calming effects on the central nervous system may promote deeper, more restorative sleep.

Small trials report shorter sleep latency (time to fall asleep) and fewer nighttime awakenings.

Enhanced Physical Performance

Resistance-trained men taking 500 mg daily for 8 weeks saw greater gains in muscle strength and size compared to placebo.

Cognitive Function and Memory

Early research suggests ashwagandha may support executive function, attention and memory, likely through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory actions in the brain.

One study of adults with mild cognitive impairment noted modest improvements in task performance over 8 weeks.

Hormonal Balance and Sexual Health

In men, ashwagandha supplementation (300–600 mg/day) has been linked to higher testosterone levels, better sperm quality and increased libido.

Limited data hint at benefits for women's sexual function, but more research is needed.

Ashwagandha Safety: Side Effects Most People Don't Expect

While ashwagandha is generally well tolerated, doctors warn of several potential side effects and interactions you may not realize.

1. Gastrointestinal Upset

Symptoms: Diarrhea, stomach cramps, nausea or vomiting.

Why it happens: High doses or poor-quality extracts may irritate the gut lining.

What doctors advise: Start with a low dose (e.g., 250 mg/day) and take with food. If upset persists, pause use and discuss with your healthcare provider.

2. Sedation and Dizziness

Symptoms: Mild drowsiness, lightheadedness or slowed reaction times.

Why it happens: Ashwagandha's calming effect on the nervous system can enhance sedation, especially if taken with other sedatives (e.g., benzodiazepines, alcohol).

What doctors advise: Avoid driving or operating heavy machinery until you know how you react. Don't combine with prescription sleep aids without medical supervision.

3. Thyroid Hormone Alterations

Potential impact: Some studies report slight increases in T₃ and T₄ thyroid hormones.

Concern: Patients with hyperthyroidism may experience worsened symptoms (palpitations, nervousness).

Effects: Ashwagandha may lower both blood pressure and blood sugar.

Risk: Combined with antihypertensive or antidiabetic medications, this could lead to dangerously low readings.

What doctors advise: Check your levels more frequently when starting ashwagandha and adjust medications only under a physician's guidance.

5. Autoimmune Disease Considerations

Concern: Ashwagandha stimulates immune activity, which might exacerbate conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, lupus or multiple sclerosis.

What doctors advise: If you have an autoimmune disorder, consult your rheumatologist or immunologist before use.

6. Allergic Reactions

Signs: Rash, itching, swelling of face or throat, difficulty breathing.

What doctors advise: Discontinue immediately and seek medical attention if you suspect an allergy.

Optimizing Ashwagandha Safety

To reduce risks and ensure you're getting a high-quality product, doctors recommend:

Choose a reputable brand that provides third-party testing for purity, heavy metals and microbial contaminants.

Look for standardized extracts containing at least 2.5% withanolides.

Start low and go slow: Begin with 250 mg once daily, increasing gradually if well tolerated.

Time your dose: Taking ashwagandha with meals can minimize stomach upset.

Monitor for side effects: Keep a symptom diary for any new or worsening issues.

Who Should Avoid or Use Caution

Pregnant or breastfeeding women (insufficient safety data)

Children and adolescents (lack of well-established dosing guidelines)

People taking sedatives, thyroid medications, blood pressure drugs or immunosuppressants

Individuals with known allergies to nightshade plants (tomatoes, potatoes, eggplant)

Integrating Ashwagandha into Your Wellness Routine

Stress management: Combine supplementation with mindfulness, meditation or gentle exercise (yoga, walking).
