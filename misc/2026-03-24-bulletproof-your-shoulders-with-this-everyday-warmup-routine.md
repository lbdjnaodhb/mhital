# Bulletproof Your Shoulders With This Everyday Warmup Routine

You only get one set of shoulders, but if you're not careful, you'll batter the hell out of them during your workouts.

Don't let that happen. Shoulder injuries and tweaks are incredibly common in the gym, but many of them can be prevented with a smart warmup that carefully prioritizes the health of one of the most versatile (and delicate) joints in your body. It doesn't take much time—5 minutes, maybe 10 tops—to get your shoulders and surrounding muscles properly warmed up. The payoff is immense, setting you up to lift more weight, lift that weight more correctly, and most importantly, lift it pain-free. (Here's how you can end shoulder pain from lifting for good.)

All you need is a light resistance band. The purpose of this warmup is to strengthen and prep the rotator cuff muscles, which spend most of your workout properly stabilizing your shoulders and helping them maintain proper positioning when you're training with more traditional movements.

If you grab the biggest resistance band in the gym and try to train this warmup heavy, you won't activate those rotator cuff muscles, because your deltoids and trapezius muscles will do all the work. That's not your objective here.

The warmup itself is quick: For the first exercise, set up a band around a vertical pole of some sort, tuck the elbow farthest away from the pole in at your hip, then position that forearm so it's parallel to the ground. Now, rotate your shoulder and forearm away from the pole as far as you can. You're training your rotator cuff muscles to "externally rotate".

Oftentimes, this is missing if you don't train it, especially if you work a desk job where you spend a lot of time with your shoulders in an "internally rotated" position, your chest muscles short, and your stabilizing back muscles overstretched. This position can lead to your tendons getting pinched in your shoulder joints. Restoring and maintaining external rotation when you're training is critical. Do one set of 30 reps per arm.

Follow that up by turning around, again pinning that elbow in to your hip, and this time, rotating your forearm in toward your body, an internal rotation. Again, do 30 reps.

After that, work to tighten and activate your back muscles that often loosen and wind up on stretch during your office work day: Lower lats and rhomboids. These muscles are critical to helping you maintain good posture when you train, and they help pull your shoulders into proper position when you're working out, opening the joint so ligaments and tendons don't grind against bone near your chest and front shoulder area.

Do 30 lower lat pulls. Then, do 30 band pull-aparts; this may be the single best exercise for shoulder health, really strengthening your rhomboids (and helping to create middle back depth so your back looks bigger and stronger, too).

You can also do archer rows, grasping one end of the band with one hand, extending that out as if you're about to fire a bow and arrow, and while keeping the other arm bent, rowing with your shoulders. This is a good way to get your external rotators firing, and to get a good mind-muscle connection going. Do this warmup before any (and ideally, every) upper body workout. It can also be done as a standalone workout for shoulder health on off days.
