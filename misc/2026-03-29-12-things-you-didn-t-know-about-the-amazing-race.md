# 12 Things You Didn't Know About 'The Amazing Race'

The Amazing Race has been on the air since 2001, and the globe-trotting show is one of the most intriguing spectacles on TV. Eleven teams travel around the world, competing challenges, and the first to reach the final location receives a million dollars. But 18 years after the show's premiere, there are still some details that fans are curious about. Here are the 12 fun behind-the-scenes facts you didn't know about The Amazing Race.

1) It takes months to prep for a season of The Amazing Race.

In his Reddit AMA, host Phil Keoghan explained how he and the show's producers get the show together for the 21-day shoot time.

"We start researching the show months in advance and then I start working on my scripts about a month out," he explained. "The key is to give the audience some takeaway about what we're going and the things we'll be doing. So I work hard on interesting factoids since I know people love learning about the world which watching the amazing race. I love that the show not only entertains but also informs."

According to How Stuff Works, Amazing Race producer and co-creator Bertram van Munster has said the plan time is about three months, and it takes a huge team to pull it off. "We have over 2,000 pe­ople working on the show worldwide," van Munster said.

2) The Amazing Race has visited over 80 countries.

The Amazing Race has visited 89 different countries (as of season 30). Besides the United States, the most visited locales are China, India, France, Italy, and Thailand. The show has been to every continent except Antarctica.

3) Political unrest can alter The Amazing Race's plans.

The show relies on consultants to keep them up to date on which locations are safe to travel to. These consults are ex-military men or retired federal agents, and they're also there to help the show if things don't go according to plan.

After the banking system collapsed amidst some political unrest in Argentina, the show decided to skip the South American country and head to South Africa instead. And after the 2011 attack of CBS reporter Lara Logan in Egypt, the show decided that "Egypt is off the map for us right now," in the words of Keoghan.

4) Unpredictable flights can also cause problems on The Amazing Race.

"Every time we get on a plane and we go to an airport, it's like rolling the dice. Is there going to be a mechanical [problem] on the plane? Are there going to be weather delays? We've been sitting on planes with the weather closing in and a team might be on another plane that got out before the weather closed in," Keoghan said. "Now we're sitting on the ground and we know the teams are racing ahead of us. It gets really challenging. I've literally raced teams to the pit stop. One time I was literally running up to the mat while they were running from the other direction."

5) Sometimes a little white lie is needed so Amazing Race spoilers won't leak

While filming in certain locations—particularly those popular among American tourists—the Amazing Race crew has to come up with the occasionally cover story to prevent passersby from picking up on spoilers.

6) The United States isn't the only country with an Amazing Race franchise.

There have been about 14 international versions of The Amazing Race, with the first being The Amazing Race Asia. The Amazing Race Canada, The Amazing Race China, and The Amazing Race Israel all have upcoming seasons premiering some time this year.

7) There's a long list of what the Amazing Race contestants can't bring with them.

Prohibited items include money, language books, maps, most electronics, written notes, and cameras. So basically, anything that could help the teams in the race.

8) If you win a trip as a prize on The Amazing Race, you have to pay taxes on it.

Mark “Abba” Abbattista appeared on Amazing Race's 21st season, and he and his partner James LoMenzo came in first on two legs of the race, so they won two trips to Antigua and Malaysia. But as Abbattista told the AV Club, the prize isn't as valuable as it seems.

"The trips are sponsored through whatever hotel puts up the trip and Travelocity is also a sponsor, so you have a liaison you go through. When the season is over, you do a bunch of legal paperwork then they turn you over to Travelocity. The woman I dealt with there was really helpful and nice and I wound up going on the trips and, yes, you pay taxes because it’s considered income as a prize."

"So if the trip is worth $10,000, guess what? You owe Uncle Sam $3,500. And they’re not all-inclusive. Your air is paid for and your hotel and they include some other things, like massages or a snorkel trip, but all the food is not included. So you’re spending money and going to places that you’re not necessarily interested in."

9) Each Amazing Race team travels with its own crew.

The crew consists of a camera person and sound person. Keoghan said in his AMA, "The teams are responsible for purchasing tickets for their crew and if they don't have enough tickets for themselves, and their crew, then they can't go!" Apparently, on-camera the teams ask for two tickets, but off-camera they ask for four.
