Understanding GLP-1 Therapy

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) agonists mimic a natural gut hormone that:

Stimulates insulin release when blood sugar is high

Slows stomach emptying to increase fullness

Reduces appetite and cravings

While powerful, these medications work best in tandem with healthy habits. Ignoring diet, movement, stress management and follow-up care can blunt results and raise the risk of side effects.

Key GLP-1 Long-Term Success Factors

Consistent nutritional quality

Regular physical activity

Behavioral and emotional support

Ongoing monitoring and adjustment

Effective side-effect management

• Balanced meals

Lean proteins (chicken, fish, legumes)

Colorful vegetables and fruits

High-fiber whole grains

Healthy fats (olive oil, nuts, avocado)

2. Physical Activity

GLP-1 agonists can enhance energy levels and reduce fatigue, making it easier to move more. Aim for:

• Aerobic exercise

150 minutes per week of moderate intensity (brisk walking, cycling, swimming)

Or 75 minutes of vigorous activity (running, HIIT)

• Strength training

Two or more days weekly, targeting major muscle groups

Bodyweight exercises (squats, push-ups) or resistance bands

4. Monitoring and Follow-Up

Regular check-ins help you stay on track and allow your healthcare team to adjust your plan.

• Blood sugar/glucose monitoring

Follow your doctor's guidance on frequency and targets

Record trends, not just individual readings

• Weight and body measurements

Weigh weekly at the same time

Track waist circumference every month

• Medication review

Discuss dose adjustments or side effects

Coordinate with other prescriptions to avoid interactions

• Lab work

Lipid panel, kidney and liver function tests as recommended

5. Managing Side Effects

Nausea, diarrhea or mild abdominal discomfort can occur, especially during dose escalation. Strategies include:

• Slow dose titration

Increase medication dose gradually per your provider's instructions

• Meal modifications

Eat smaller, more frequent meals

Avoid fried or very fatty foods

• Hydration and electrolytes

Sip water, herbal teas or electrolyte solutions

Replace sodium lost during bouts of diarrhea

• Communication

Report persistent or severe side effects to your healthcare team
