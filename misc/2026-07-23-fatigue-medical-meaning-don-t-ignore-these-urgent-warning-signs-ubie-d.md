Fatigue is a persistent feeling of physical and mental tiredness or exhaustion that goes beyond normal sleepiness. It can make everyday activities—like working, exercising, or concentrating—feel difficult or overwhelming. Unlike ordinary tiredness that improves with rest, medical fatigue often lingers and may signal an underlying health condition, such as anemia, thyroid issues, sleep disorders, depression, or chronic illness.

Explanation

Fatigue in the medical world means feeling very tired and not having enough energy to do things. It can be caused by many things, like not getting enough sleep, working too hard, or being sick.

Types of Fatigue

Physical Fatigue: This is when your body feels tired. You might feel like your muscles are weak and you can't do things you usually do, like playing or running.

[These Urgent](https://lumina-dermis.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Mental Fatigue: This is when your mind feels tired. It might be hard to think clearly, pay attention, or remember things.

Causes of Fatigue

Lack of Sleep: Not sleeping enough can make you feel tired all the time.

Illness: Being sick, like having a cold or the flu, can make you feel worn out.

Stress: Feeling worried or stressed can also make you feel very tired.

Fatigue is different from just being sleepy. Sleepiness is when you feel like you want to go to sleep, but fatigue is when you feel like you can't do things even if you're not sleepy. If you feel very tired for a long time, it's important to use a free symptom checker to help identify what might be causing your fatigue and determine if you should see a doctor.
