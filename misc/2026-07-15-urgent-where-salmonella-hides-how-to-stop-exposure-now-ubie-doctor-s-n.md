In what environments is salmonella most commonly found, and how can exposure be minimized?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Salmonella is a bacteria commonly found on farms and in animals like chickens, pigs, and cattle. It can spread to humans through contaminated food, water, or contact with infected animals. To reduce your risk of Salmonella infection, follow these key prevention steps:

Practice good hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly with soap after handling animals, using the restroom, or preparing food.

Cook food thoroughly: Ensure poultry, eggs, and meat reach safe internal temperatures to kill bacteria.

Salmonella bacteria are often found in places where animals like chickens and pigs are raised. These bacteria can spread to humans through food, especially meat and eggs. If you're experiencing symptoms that concern you, consider using a free symptom checker to better understand your condition.

Ways to Minimize Exposure:

Cook Thoroughly: Make sure to cook poultry and eggs until they are well done. This kills any Salmonella that might be present.

Practice Good Hygiene: Always wash your hands with soap and water after handling raw meat or eggs.

Safe Food Handling: Keep raw meat separate from other foods to avoid cross-contamination.
