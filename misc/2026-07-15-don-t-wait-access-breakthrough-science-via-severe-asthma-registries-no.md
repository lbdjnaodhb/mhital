Explanation

Severe asthma affects a small but significant portion of people with asthma. It often requires specialized treatments and careful monitoring. By taking part in severe asthma registries, patients and healthcare providers contribute to a richer understanding of the disease and speed the development of new therapies. Below, we explore how registries work, their link to clinical trials registration, and why your involvement matters.

What Are Severe Asthma Registries?

Severe asthma registries are organized databases that collect detailed information on patients whose asthma remains uncontrolled despite high‐level therapy. These registries are typically run by academic research centers, professional medical societies, or health authorities. Their goals include:

Tracking patient characteristics, treatment histories, and outcomes

Identifying patterns in symptoms, triggers, and responses to therapy

Supporting research into new drugs, biomarkers, and personalized treatment strategies

By pooling data across clinics, regions, or even countries, registries overcome the limitations of small, single-center studies. They create a real-world evidence base grounded in everyday clinical practice.

How Registries Fuel Breakthrough Science

Data Standardization and QualityRegistry coordinators use uniform definitions (for example, per Global Initiative for Asthma guidelines) and clear data entry protocols. This ensures that information from different sites can be compared reliably.

Rich PhenotypingRegistries capture not just lung function tests, but also detailed patient histories, allergen sensitivities, blood markers (like eosinophil counts), and quality-of-life measures. This deep phenotyping helps researchers identify subtypes of severe asthma that may respond differently to targeted therapies.

Hypothesis GenerationLarge-scale data spur new ideas. For instance, clustering analyses of registry patients have suggested novel mechanisms of airway inflammation, leading to investigational drug programs.

Optimizing Existing TherapiesBy observing real-world outcomes, registries can reveal which biologics or add-on treatments work best for particular patient profiles. This information guides physicians in tailoring therapies more effectively.

Linking Severe Asthma Registries and Clinical Trials Registration

Clinical trials registration is the process of publicly listing trial details—such as objectives, eligibility criteria, and locations—before a study begins. Registries and clinical trials work hand in hand:

Patient IdentificationSevere asthma registries serve as a pool of well-characterized patients. Researchers can screen registry participants for potential enrollment in interventional studies, accelerating recruitment.

Transparency and TrustRegistering trials on publicly accessible platforms (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov or national equivalents) reduces publication bias and lets patients see all ongoing research. Linking registry data to registered trials ensures that participants know how their information may lead to new therapies.

Regulatory ComplianceMany countries require clinical trials registration to satisfy ethical and legal standards. Registries that collaborate with trial sponsors help streamline approval processes by providing pre-collected baseline data.

How to Get Involved

Ask Your SpecialistTalk with your pulmonologist, allergist, or asthma specialist. Many centers are already affiliated with national or international registries.

Check Professional Society WebsitesOrganizations like the European Respiratory Society or American Thoracic Society often list ongoing registry projects and contact details.

Complete the Enrollment ProcessYou may be asked to provide:

Medical history and current medications

Recent lung function tests (e.g., FEV₁)

Laboratory results (blood eosinophils, immunoglobulin E)

Questionnaires on symptoms, triggers, and quality of life

Consent and Data PrivacyYou will sign an informed consent form explaining how your data will be used, stored, and shared in de-identified form.

Stay EngagedPeriodic follow-up visits or online surveys keep your information current and enrich the registry's value.

Real-World Impact: Case Examples

A global severe asthma registry identified a subset of patients with high eosinophil counts who responded exceptionally well to a new monoclonal antibody. Subsequent clinical trial registration and rapid recruitment led to the drug's approval within two years.

Data from a national registry revealed that patients exposed to a particular environmental trigger had worse outcomes. This insight prompted a follow-up trial, now listed in a public registry, exploring an add-on therapy to counteract that specific inflammation pathway.

Worsening breathlessness

Frequent hospitalizations or emergency visits
