Chronic indigestion—often experienced as bloating, heartburn, burping, or a persistent "full" feeling after meals—affects millions of people worldwide. Apple cider vinegar (ACV) has become a popular home remedy for digestive complaints. Understanding Chronic Indigestion

Chronic indigestion, or dyspepsia, is characterized by discomfort in the upper abdomen that persists for weeks or months. Common triggers include:

High-fat or spicy foods

Overeating

Stress and anxiety

Certain medications (e.g., NSAIDs)

Underlying conditions (e.g., gastritis, ulcers, gallstones)

While occasional indigestion is generally harmless, chronic symptoms should never be ignored.

How to Use ACV Safely

If you decide to try ACV for chronic indigestion, follow these guidelines:

Dosage and Timing

Start low: 1 teaspoon (5 mL) of raw, unfiltered ACV in 8 ounces of water.

Increase gradually over 1–2 weeks, up to 1–2 tablespoons (15–30 mL) per day.

Best taken 15–20 minutes before meals to prime your stomach acid production.

Preparation Tips

Always dilute ACV in water to protect tooth enamel and your esophagus.

Add a squeeze of lemon or a dash of cinnamon for flavor without extra sugar.

Drink through a straw to minimize contact with teeth.

Precautions

Do not exceed recommended dosages. Excessive acetic acid can erode enamel, lower potassium levels, or worsen acid reflux in sensitive individuals.

Avoid undiluted ACV—it's corrosive and may burn the mouth or throat.

People with gastroparesis, severe GERD, or a history of peptic ulcers should consult a healthcare professional before use.

If you're on medications (e.g., diuretics, insulin), talk to your doctor. ACV can interact with certain prescriptions.
