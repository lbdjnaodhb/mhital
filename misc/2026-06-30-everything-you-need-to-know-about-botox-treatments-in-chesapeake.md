What is Botox?

Botox is a purified form of botulinum toxin, a protein that temporarily relaxes the muscles it is injected into. By targeting specific facial muscles, Botox prevents them from contracting, which can smooth out the skin and reduce the appearance of wrinkles and fine lines. It is commonly used for cosmetic purposes but also has medical uses, such as treating excessive sweating, chronic migraines, and certain muscular disorders.

How Botox Works: The Science Behind the Treatment

Botox works by temporarily blocking nerve signals to the muscles. The toxin blocks the release of acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter responsible for muscle contraction. This action relaxes the targeted muscles, preventing them from contracting and causing wrinkles. The results can last for three to four months, after which another treatment is necessary to maintain the smooth, youthful look.
