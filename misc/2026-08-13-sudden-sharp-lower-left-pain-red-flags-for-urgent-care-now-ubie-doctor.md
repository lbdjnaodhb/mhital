Sudden Sharp Pain in the Lower Left Side: When to Seek Urgent Care

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sudden sharp pain in the lower left abdomen has many possible causes. Common ones include gas or muscle strain, but more urgent conditions—such as diverticulitis, kidney stones, ovarian torsion, ectopic pregnancy, bowel obstruction, or perforation—also present this way.

Seek urgent care immediately if you have severe pain, fever, persistent vomiting, blood in stool or urine, black stools, fainting, dizziness, a rigid abdomen, pregnancy, or pain following injury.

If your pain is mild with no red-flag symptoms, brief home monitoring is reasonable. However, any pain that persists, worsens, or returns should be evaluated by a clinician.

Explanation

Experiencing a sharp pain in lower left abdomen can be unsettling. Sometimes it's caused by something minor, like gas or muscle strain. Other times, it may signal a more serious condition that needs urgent medical care.

Understanding what could be behind the pain — and knowing when to act — can help you make informed decisions without unnecessary panic.

What Organs Are in the Lower Left Abdomen?

The lower left area of your abdomen contains several important structures, including:

The lower part of the large intestine (colon)

The sigmoid colon

Part of the small intestine

The left ovary and fallopian tube (in women)

The left ureter (tube connecting kidney to bladder)

Muscles and connective tissue of the abdominal wall

Because multiple organs are involved, a sharp pain in lower left abdomen can have several possible causes.

Common Causes of Sharp Pain in Lower Left

Not all pain is dangerous. In many cases, the cause is temporary and treatable.

1. Gas or Constipation

Gas buildup or stool trapped in the colon can cause:

Sharp or cramping pain

Bloating

Relief after passing gas or having a bowel movement

This type of pain often comes and goes.

2. Diverticulitis

One of the most common causes of sharp pain in lower left adults — especially over age 40 — is diverticulitis.

Diverticula are small pouches that can form in the colon. If they become inflamed or infected, symptoms may include:

Diverticulitis requires medical evaluation. Mild cases may be treated with antibiotics, but severe cases may require hospitalization.

3. Kidney Stones

A kidney stone moving through the left ureter can cause:

Sudden, intense sharp pain in lower left or lower back

Pain that radiates to the groin

Nausea or vomiting

Blood in the urine

Kidney stone pain is often severe and comes in waves.

4. Ovarian Causes (Women)

Women may experience sharp lower left abdominal pain due to:

Ovarian cyst

Ovarian torsion (twisting of the ovary)

Ectopic pregnancy

6. Hernia

A hernia occurs when tissue pushes through a weak spot in the abdominal wall. Symptoms may include:

Sharp pain in lower left that worsens when coughing or lifting

A visible or palpable bulge

Pressure or heaviness

Some hernias require surgical repair.

When Is Sharp Pain in Lower Left an Emergency?

While many causes are not life-threatening, certain symptoms mean you should seek urgent medical care immediately.

Go to the ER or seek urgent care if you have:

Sudden, severe, unbearable pain

Fever over 101°F (38.3°C)

Persistent vomiting

Blood in stool or vomit

Black, tarry stools

Fainting or dizziness

Rigid or hard abdomen

Pain after an injury

Sharp pain in lower left during pregnancy

When Can You Monitor Symptoms at Home?

You may consider monitoring symptoms for 24–48 hours if:

The pain is mild

You have no fever

There is no vomiting

You can eat and drink normally

The pain improves with rest or bowel movement

However, if the pain persists, worsens, or new symptoms develop, contact a healthcare provider.

How Doctors Evaluate Sharp Pain in Lower Left

When you see a doctor, they may:

Ask about timing and severity of pain

Check for fever

Perform a physical exam

Order blood tests

Request imaging (CT scan, ultrasound, or X-ray)

Imaging is often used to diagnose diverticulitis, kidney stones, ovarian issues, or bowel problems.

Be prepared to describe:

When the pain started

Whether it's constant or comes in waves

What makes it better or worse

Any changes in bowel or urinary habits

Special Considerations for Women

Women with sharp pain in lower left abdomen should always consider reproductive causes.

Seek urgent care if you have:

A positive pregnancy test and abdominal pain

Sudden severe pelvic pain

Pain with dizziness or fainting

Abnormal vaginal bleeding

Ectopic pregnancy can rupture and cause life-threatening bleeding. Early treatment saves lives.

Pain That Should Never Be Ignored

Even if symptoms seem mild, certain patterns require prompt evaluation:

Pain lasting more than a few days

Recurrent episodes of sharp pain

Pain with unexplained weight loss

Pain with persistent bowel changes

Pain that wakes you from sleep

Chronic or recurring pain may indicate inflammatory bowel disease, chronic diverticular disease, or other conditions that benefit from early treatment.

Practical Steps While Waiting for Care

If symptoms are mild and you are monitoring at home:

Stay hydrated

Avoid heavy meals

Rest

Avoid strenuous activity

Use over-the-counter pain relievers cautiously (avoid NSAIDs if stomach issues are suspected unless advised by a doctor)

Do not apply heat if you suspect infection or inflammation until evaluated.

The Bottom Line

A sharp pain in lower left abdomen can range from minor digestive issues to serious medical emergencies.
