# Breast Lump: When to Worry? Don’t Ignore These 5 Red Flags | Ubie Doctor's Note

When should I be concerned about a lump in my breast?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

[Red Flags](https://nootropic-atlas.escortskhi.com/)

Most breast lumps are not cancerous, but you should be concerned if a lump feels hard, is growing in size, doesn't move when touched, or causes skin changes such as dimpling, redness, or nipple discharge. Any new, persistent, or unusual breast lump should be evaluated by a doctor promptly, especially if you have a family history of breast cancer or are over 40.

Explanation

When you find a lump in your breast, it can be worrisome. Most breast lumps are not cancerous, but it's important to know when to see a doctor.

When to be Concerned:

Hard or Firm Lumps: If the lump feels very hard or firm, it might need more attention.

Growing Lumps: If the lump is getting bigger over time, it's a good idea to get it checked.

[When to Worry](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/6792819724.html)

Skin Changes: Look for changes in the skin over the lump, like dimpling or redness.

Pain or Tenderness: Some lumps can be painful. Pain alone doesn't mean it's serious, but it's worth checking out.
