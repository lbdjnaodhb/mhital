Pain that radiates from the buttock to the front of the thigh is most often caused by sacroiliac (SI) joint dysfunction or nerve entrapment in the hip region, such as compression of the femoral or lateral femoral cutaneous nerve. SI joint problems can trigger referred pain along the front of the leg, while pinched nerves in the hip can cause burning, tingling, or aching that follows the nerve's path. Other possible causes include hip joint arthritis, lumbar spine issues, or muscle strain.

Because these conditions share overlapping symptoms but require very different treatments, identifying the underlying cause early is essential to avoid worsening pain or long-term nerve damage. Pain that travels from the buttock to the front of the thigh can have several causes. One possible cause is a problem with the sacroiliac joint, which is where the spine meets the pelvis. This joint can become painful and affect the legs, as explained by Murakami and colleagues.

Another potential cause is nerve entrapment in the hip region. Nerves can get pinched or trapped, leading to pain that spreads to the thigh, as discussed by McCrory and Bell.

Finally, Chamberlain highlights that hip pain in adults can have many causes and should be carefully evaluated to determine the exact reason for the pain.
