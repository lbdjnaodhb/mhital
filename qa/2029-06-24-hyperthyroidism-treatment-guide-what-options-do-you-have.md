# Hyperthyroidism Treatment Guide: What Options Do You have?

Hyperthyroidism Treatment Guide: What Options Do You have?

An active thyroid can dramatically affect your quality of life if left untreated. It has a variety of causes and risk factors associated with it and impacts how the body uses energy. Many treatment methods can help those with hyperthyroidism.

What is Hyperthyroidism?

The thyroid, an organ of the endocrine system, is located in the front of your neck and produces two hormones. These hormones regulate how food is converted into energy, body temperature, heartbeat, and weight. Many thyroid disorders exist, but Hyperthyroidism occurs when the thyroid gland produces more hormones than your body requires.

What causes Hyperthyroidism?

There are many causes of hyperthyroidism. Some of the more common causes of hyperthyroidism include:

Graves disease: An autoimmune disorder where your immune system attacks the thyroid, resulting in an increased production of hormones.

Nodules: Overactive thyroid modules are more frequently found in older adults and can contribute to an increase in thyroid production.

Inflammation: If the thyroid is inflamed, which can occur with certain medications or illnesses, it can cause hyperthyroidism.

Some less common causes of hyperthyroidism include consuming too much iodine from medical imaging or foods, noncancerous growths, or tumors in the testes/ovaries.

You may be at a higher risk of developing hyperthyroidism if:

You have a medical condition that affects your thyroid, such as lupus or type 1 diabetes

You take medication that is high in iodine

There is a family history of thyroid disease

You have been pregnant in the last six months

You are an adult over the age of 60

You use nicotine products

Hyperthyroidism Symptoms

Symptoms of hyperthyroidism vary from person to person and may be affected by age. Older adults may experience loss of appetite or withdrawal from social situations, and many of these symptoms can be misconstrued as dementia or depression. Getting an accurate diagnosis from a physician is essential to rule out other disorders.

Common symptoms of hyperthyroidism include:

Quick or irregular heartbeat

Fatigue

An enlarged neck, also known as a goiter

Frequent bowel movements

Nervousness or irritability

How is Hyperthyroidism Diagnosed?

If you suspect you have a thyroid issue, schedule an appointment to see your primary care physician. Often, symptoms can also be signs of another condition.

There are three main tests used to diagnose hyperthyroidism:

Blood tests: Used to determine if the thyroid is working properly by analyzing hormones. This can also be used to diagnose hypothyroidism, Graves disease, thyroid cancer, or Hashimoto’s disease.

Imaging tests: Your provider may choose to do an ultrasound, which uses sound waves to look at body tissues and turn them into images. Radiation is not used in this test.

Physical exam: Your physician will study any areas of pain or inflammation, particularly the neck, for any goiters.

What are the Treatment Options for Hyperthyroidism?

Treatments will vary based on what is determined to be the cause of hyperthyroidism. Your physician will first determine the cause and severity of your thyroid. There are three primary treatment modalities.

Medication

Common medications for hyperthyroidism include beta-blockers and antithyroid medications.1 Beta-blockers work by opening up the blood vessels and can reduce symptoms caused by hyperthyroidism, but they do not stop the thyroid from producing hormones. Antithyroid medications are also common and may include medications like Methimazole or Propylthiouracil. These act by telling the thyroid to make fewer hormones but are more likely to produce side effects such as allergic reactions. Although they may take time to work, they often assist with symptom management.

Radioiodine Therapy

Radioiodine treatment is common for hyperthyroidism and can be done as a capsule or a liquid. This treatment destroys the cells that produce thyroid hormones and does not impact other tissues. You may need several treatments to manage the condition, but often this is used in conjunction with medications to make treatments more comfortable. Radioiodine therapy may cause the individual to get hypothyroidism, but this is often considered a worthwhile approach since hypothyroidism is easier to treat. Radioiodine treatment is not used for pregnant or breastfeeding women.

Surgery

Surgery is less common than other treatment methods but may be required for those who have difficulty taking medications or are pregnant. You may develop hypothyroidism after surgery, but this is more manageable long-term than hyperthyroidism. Two types of surgeries are commonly used; a total thyroidectomy and a thyroid lobectomy with isthmusectomy. The former makes a small incision in the neck and removes the entire thyroid gland, whereas a thyroid lobectomy with isthmusectomy will only remove half of the thyroid.

Hyperthyroidism Treatment at Home vs. a Clinic

Some physicians will do home visits for those who have issues with transportation and scheduling or simply feel too ill to leave their homes. Medical house calls can range from primary care, wound care, physical therapy, and more, which provides many options for those struggling to manage other comorbidities. It can also help ensure patients stick to their treatment plan.

Remember that not every visiting physician can do all the required treatments, which may mean the patient may be referred for imaging or lab work outside the home. In a clinic, most of the services can be completed on-site, and you may have more access to various staff members. If you feel at-home services are needed, speak to your primary care physician to find someone in the area who can help.

Lab Testing - Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it important to do lab tests occasionally?

It is important to do lab tests occasionally because they can provide valuable information about an individual's health and help to identify potential health issues early on. Lab tests can measure a wide range of factors, including blood count, cholesterol levels, liver and kidney function, and hormone levels, and can provide insight into an individual's overall health and wellness. Additionally, lab tests can help to diagnose and monitor the progression of certain medical conditions, such as diabetes and heart disease, and can help to identify any potential health risks or concerns. By doing lab tests occasionally, individuals can take proactive steps to maintain their health and wellbeing and reduce the risk of potential health problems in the future.

What does a routine blood test cover?

A routine blood test is used to check for a range of things, including your blood count and the levels of certain chemicals and substances in your blood. Blood tests can also be used to check how well certain organs, such as your liver and kidneys, are functioning.

How is a blood sample collected for lab testing?

A blood sample for lab testing is typically collected through a process called venipuncture, which involves inserting a small needle into a vein to draw blood. This is usually done on the inside of the elbow or the back of the hand.

What is the cost of a lab test?

In general, the cost of a lab test can range from a few dollars to several hundred dollars. It is always best to consult with your doctor or healthcare provider to get an accurate estimate of the cost of a lab test.
