What is the clinical definition of being fatigued?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Fatigue is persistent tiredness or lack of energy that doesn't improve with rest and can interfere with daily activities, work, and overall quality of life. Unlike normal tiredness, fatigue may signal an underlying condition such as anemia, thyroid disorders, sleep apnea, depression, or chronic illness.

Explanation

Fatigue is described as a persistent feeling of tiredness or lack of energy that does not improve with rest. It is different from just feeling sleepy or drowsy. Fatigue can affect a person's ability to do everyday activities and can be caused by various factors, including physical, mental, or medical conditions.

Key Points:

Difference from Sleepiness:

Sleepiness is the need to sleep, while fatigue is a lack of energy.

Fatigue can occur even after a good night's sleep.
