Explanation

If you've noticed a small lump at the bottom of your breastbone — and it feels tender, sore, or suddenly more noticeable — you're not alone. Many people discover their xiphoid process by accident and immediately worry something is wrong.

In most cases, this small structure is completely normal. But pain or swelling in the area can sometimes signal irritation, injury, or an underlying medical issue. Here's what you need to know — clearly and calmly — about the xiphoid process, why it may hurt, and what to do next.

What Is the Xiphoid Process?

The xiphoid process is a small, pointed piece of cartilage (which often turns to bone in adulthood) located at the very bottom of your sternum (breastbone). You can usually feel it where your ribs meet in the center of your chest, just above the upper abdomen.

It is the smallest part of the sternum.

It starts as cartilage and typically hardens (ossifies) with age.

Its size and shape vary from person to person.

It serves as an attachment point for abdominal muscles and the diaphragm.

For some people, the xiphoid process sticks out slightly. For others, it curves inward. Both can be normal variations.

Why Does the Xiphoid Process Hurt?

Pain in this area is sometimes called xiphodynia or xiphoid syndrome. It's not very common, but it is recognized in medical literature.

Here are the most common reasons your xiphoid process may hurt:

1. Inflammation (Xiphodynia)

This is irritation or inflammation of the xiphoid process and surrounding tissues.

Symptoms may include:

Localized tenderness when you press on it

Sharp or dull pain in the lower chest

Pain that worsens with bending, twisting, or heavy lifting

Discomfort that may radiate to the back, shoulders, or abdomen

Inflammation can develop after:

Repetitive strain

Intense core workouts

Heavy lifting

Direct trauma

Persistent coughing

3. Muscle Strain

Because abdominal muscles attach near the xiphoid process, straining those muscles can cause pain in the same area.

Common triggers include:

Intense ab workouts

Sudden twisting movements

Chronic coughing

4. Gastrointestinal Issues

Sometimes pain near the xiphoid process isn't coming from the bone at all.

Conditions like:

Acid reflux (GERD)

Gastritis

Peptic ulcers

can cause upper abdominal discomfort that feels like it's centered around the lower sternum.

If you also have:

Burning in the chest

Sour taste in your mouth

Nausea

Pain after eating

the cause may be digestive rather than skeletal.

5. Hernia

In rare cases, what feels like a lump near the xiphoid process could be a small epigastric hernia, where tissue pushes through the abdominal wall.

This usually:

Feels soft

May change size when straining

Can cause discomfort with coughing or lifting

6. Serious Causes (Less Common, But Important)

Pain in the lower sternum area can sometimes mimic:

Heart-related chest pain

Gallbladder disease

Pancreatitis

If pain is severe, unexplained, or accompanied by other concerning symptoms, it must be evaluated promptly.

When Is a Lump Normal?

In many cases, what feels like a lump is simply your normal xiphoid process.

It may feel more prominent if you:

Lose weight

Improve posture

Build abdominal muscle

Age (as cartilage turns to bone)

A normal xiphoid process:

Feels firm

Does not rapidly grow

Is centered in the midline

Is not associated with systemic symptoms (fever, unexplained weight loss)

Symptoms That Require Immediate Medical Attention

Do not ignore chest-area pain if you experience:

Chest pressure or tightness

Pain spreading to the arm, jaw, or back

Shortness of breath

Sweating or nausea with chest pain

Sudden severe abdominal pain

Fever with worsening tenderness

A lump that is rapidly growing

These symptoms could signal a life-threatening condition. Call emergency services immediately.

How Doctors Diagnose Xiphoid Process Pain

If you visit a healthcare professional, they may:

Perform a physical exam (pressing on the xiphoid process to reproduce pain)

Ask about recent injuries or exercise

Evaluate for gastrointestinal symptoms

Order imaging (X-ray, ultrasound, or CT scan) if needed

In many cases, diagnosis is clinical — meaning it's based on your symptoms and exam findings.

Medically Approved Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the cause.

For Inflammation or Strain:

Rest from aggravating activities

Over-the-counter anti-inflammatory medications (if safe for you)

Ice packs for 15–20 minutes at a time

Gentle stretching after acute pain improves

For Acid Reflux or Digestive Causes:

Avoid trigger foods (spicy, fatty, acidic)

Eat smaller meals

Avoid lying down right after eating

Consider medical therapy if recommended by your doctor

For Persistent Xiphodynia:

In rare chronic cases, a physician may recommend:

Physical therapy

Local anesthetic or steroid injections

Further imaging studies

Surgery to remove the xiphoid process is extremely rare and only considered in severe, treatment-resistant cases.

What You Should Not Do

Do not repeatedly press or "check" the area — this can worsen irritation.

Do not assume all chest pain is harmless.

Do not self-diagnose if symptoms are severe or worsening.

Can Stress Make It Worse?

Yes. Stress can increase muscle tension, alter posture, and worsen acid reflux — all of which may increase discomfort around the xiphoid process.

Improving posture, stretching your chest and upper abdomen, and practicing stress reduction techniques can sometimes help.

The Bottom Line

The xiphoid process is a normal anatomical structure that can sometimes feel like a lump — especially if you've never noticed it before.

Most causes of xiphoid process pain are:

Benign

Treatable

Related to strain, inflammation, or digestion

However, chest and upper abdominal pain should never be ignored if it is severe, persistent, or associated with other concerning symptoms.
