Internal Medicine

Knee pain when walking down stairs is often caused by joint problems, weak muscles, or instability that make it difficult to safely control your leg movements. Common culprits include patellofemoral pain syndrome ("runner's knee"), osteoarthritis, meniscus tears, and quadriceps weakness. Descending stairs places up to 3.5 times your body weight on the knee joint, which is why symptoms often appear going down rather than up.

Because knee pain has many possible causes—ranging from minor muscle imbalances to conditions requiring medical treatment—identifying the specific source is the first step toward relief. A free, instant, online symptom check can help you understand what may be causing your knee pain and guide you on the best next steps, whether that's targeted exercises, home care, or seeing a specialist. When you have knee problems like osteoarthritis or patellofemoral pain syndrome, your knee joint can hurt and become stiff. This pain can make it harder for you to bend your knee and support your body weight properly when stepping down. Walking down stairs is more demanding than walking on flat ground because your knee and surrounding muscles must control the drop while keeping you stable.

[Walking Downstairs](https://satietybrief.china-akan.com/)

If you change your gait due to chronic pain, it may lead to a less stable gait; this may make walking down the stairs difficult. If you're experiencing persistent knee pain or difficulty with stairs, consider using a free symptom checker to better understand your symptoms. All these factors add up so that descending stairs becomes a difficult task, and taking extra care, using handrails, or even limiting stair use might be needed to stay safe.
