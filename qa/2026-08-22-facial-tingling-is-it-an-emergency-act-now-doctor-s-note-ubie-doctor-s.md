What are the potential causes of facial tingling?

Internal Medicine

Facial tingling, also known as facial paresthesia, is often caused by nerve-related conditions such as trigeminal neuropathy or neurocutaneous dysesthesias, which affect the sensory nerves in the face. In some cases, facial tingling may signal a more serious cardiovascular issue, such as a stroke, especially when it appears suddenly or on one side of the face.

Because facial tingling can stem from causes ranging from minor nerve irritation to medical emergencies, identifying the underlying reason quickly is essential. [Facial Tingling Is](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/)

Facial tingling is often linked to nerve problems in the face. One common cause is trigeminal neuropathy, which affects the trigeminal nerve responsible for sensation in the face. This can lead to tingling, numbness, or pain.

Another possible cause is neurocutaneous dysesthesias, which are conditions affecting the skin and nerves. These can result in unusual sensations on the skin, including tingling.

Tingling to one side of the face may also be a sign of a stroke, which requires emergency medical evaluation.

If you're experiencing facial tingling, you can use a free symptom checker to help identify potential causes, but a healthcare professional should evaluate any new, persistent or concerning symptoms.
