What could cause a long stringy thing in my poop?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Long, stringy stools can result from several causes, ranging from harmless dietary factors to underlying digestive conditions. Common explanations include high-fiber foods, mucus in the stool, mild constipation, or intestinal narrowing. In some cases, stringy stools may signal irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), inflammation, infection, or, less commonly, more serious gastrointestinal issues that warrant medical attention.

Because stool shape and texture can reflect what's happening inside your digestive tract, persistent changes shouldn't be ignored. Identifying the cause early helps you take the right next steps—whether that's a simple dietary adjustment or a conversation with your doctor.
