# Is green discharge during pregnancy normal or worrisome? | Ubie Doctor's Note

Is green discharge during pregnancy normal or worrisome?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Green discharge during pregnancy can be either normal or a warning sign of infection. A faint greenish tint that is thin, mild or odorless, and not accompanied by itching or pain may be a harmless hormonal variation worth monitoring briefly. However, bright green, frothy, heavy, or foul-smelling discharge—especially with itching, burning, pelvic pain, fever, or bleeding—often signals an infection such as trichomoniasis, gonorrhea, or chlamydia. These infections require prompt medical evaluation to protect both you and your baby, and treatment typically involves pregnancy-safe antibiotics after lab testing.

Because untreated infections during pregnancy can lead to preterm labor, low birth weight, or transmission to your baby, it's critical to identify the cause quickly. Explanation

Green discharge during pregnancy can be unsettling, but it isn't always a sign of a serious problem. Pregnancy hormones cause many changes in your body—including your vaginal discharge—so it's important to understand what's normal and when to seek help.

Why vaginal discharge changes in pregnancy

• Hormonal shifts (especially higher estrogen) increase blood flow to the vagina and stimulate more secretions.• Normal pregnancy discharge (leucorrhea) is often thin, milky or clear, and mild-smelling.• Its main purpose is to keep the birth canal clean and reduce infection risk.

When green discharge is worrisome

Bright green, frothy or heavy discharge—often with other symptoms—usually indicates infection. Common causes include:

1. Trichomoniasis

Protozoan parasite, sexually transmitted

Discharge: frothy, yellow-green, fishy odor

Other symptoms: itching, redness, painful urination

Treatment: metronidazole or tinidazole per CDC STD Treatment Guidelines (Workowski & Bolan, 2015)

2. Gonorrhea

Neisseria gonorrhoeae infection

Discharge: purulent, greenish, may be sticky

Other symptoms: pelvic pain, burning on urination, risk of spread to uterus/fallopian tubes

Treatment: intramuscular ceftriaxone plus oral azithromycin per guidelines

3. Chlamydia

Often co-exists with gonorrhea

Treatment principles in pregnancy

• Many standard antibiotics (e.g., metronidazole, ceftriaxone, azithromycin) are safe in pregnancy when used as directed.• Always follow dosing and duration exactly.• Treating sexual partners is crucial for STDs to prevent reinfection.

Self-care and prevention tips

Wash daily with mild, unscented soap and water

Avoid douching, scented sprays or harsh soaps

Change underwear if it becomes damp; choose breathable fabrics

Practice safe sex (latex condoms) to prevent STDs

Keep follow-up appointments and finish any prescribed medications

When to worry and seek immediate help

Green discharge paired with any of these signs warrants urgent evaluation:

High fever

Severe abdominal pain

Heavy vaginal bleeding

Foul-smelling discharge with tissue debris

These could indicate serious infections, miscarriage or other complications.
